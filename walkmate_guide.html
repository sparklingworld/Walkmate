WalkMate Architecture & Core Concepts

A comprehensive deep-dive into how WalkMate was conceived, architected, and built—from real-time GPS trigonometry and MET exercise science to Japanese editorial Polaroid memory cards.

Platform: Flutter (Android) Language: Dart Architecture: Service-Oriented Reactive MVC Privacy: 100% Zero-Route Storage

1. Philosophy: Why WalkMate is Different

Modern fitness applications (such as Strava, Nike Run Club, or Apple Fitness) are built around competition, public leaderboards, pace pressure, and route bragging. For everyday mindful walkers, this creates anxiety rather than wellness.

WalkMate was built with a fundamentally opposite paradigm:

  • No Destination or Route Required: You don't need to "plan a track". Step outside your door and take 300 meters or 4 kilometers. Distance is purely cumulative.
  • Absolute Privacy by Architecture: Most fitness apps secretly upload your breadcrumb GPS route to cloud servers. WalkMate never stores coordinates in memory or on disk. Once a delta is computed, the latitude and longitude are immediately dropped.
  • Calming Botanical Aesthetics: Instead of high-voltage neon red or aggressive athletic blacks, WalkMate uses Japanese forest bathing (Shinrin-yoku) tones: Sage Green, Oat Sand, and Peach Blossom.
  • Non-Competitive Milestone Cheers: Every 500 meters, you receive a gentle word of gratitude and body-mind scientific insights instead of a speed comparison.
🔒 The Zero-Route Privacy Guarantee

In WalkMate, the GPS stream only exists during the active walk. The app computes: delta = distance(previousLat, previousLng, currentLat, currentLng). Once cumulativeDistance += delta executes, previousLat/Lng are overwritten. No list of points, GeoJSON, or map polylines are ever constructed or saved.

2. Flutter Foundations: How the Code Works

To understand WalkMate, you only need to grasp three core concepts of the Flutter framework:

1. "Everything is a Widget"

In Flutter, buttons, text, paddings, animations, and entire screens are all Widgets nested together in a tree hierarchy. When data changes, Flutter redraws only the specific sub-tree.

2. Stateless vs. Stateful

StatelessWidget is static (like a badge or button). StatefulWidget holds dynamic state (like a timer ticking, weight input, or active walk metrics) via setState().

State Management with ChangeNotifier

Instead of using heavy, bloated state management libraries, WalkMate uses Flutter's built-in ChangeNotifier. Our TrackingService extends ChangeNotifier. When GPS moves or the 1-second timer ticks, it calls notifyListeners(). The UI screen listens and updates seamlessly at 60 FPS without freezing the main thread.

3. Deep Dive into Every Feature & Concept

3.1 One-Time Weight Persistence (StorageService)

Concept: Why ask for weight? Energy expenditure in walking is directly proportional to body mass. Moving a 90 kg mass 1 kilometer requires significantly more energy than moving a 55 kg mass.

  • Package Used: shared_preferences (Local key-value store using Android's native SharedPreferences).
  • Normalized Storage: Weight is always stored internally in kilograms (kg). If a user enters 154 lbs, the app converts and stores 70.0 kg:
    weightKg = displayWeight / 2.20462.
  • Privacy Reassurance: Data remains in the application's private sandboxed data directory on the Android device. Zero cloud sync.

3.2 GPS Tracking & Cumulative Distance Math (TrackingService)

Concept: How does temporary GPS tracking work without logging routes?

  • Package: geolocator (interfaces with Android's FusedLocationProviderClient).
  • The Distance Filter (Anti-Jitter): When standing still, GPS coordinates drift naturally by 1–5 meters due to atmospheric interference. If recorded raw, the user would accumulate "ghost distance". WalkMate sets distanceFilter: 3 meters and requires accuracy under 35m to accept a coordinate.
  • Spherical Trigonometry (Haversine Formula): The distance between two GPS points on Earth's curved surface is calculated using the spherical law of cosines:
a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat1) · cos(lat2) · sin²(Δlon/2)
c = 2 · atan2(√a, √(1−a))
Distance (meters) = EarthRadius (6,371,000m) · c
  • Speed & Teleport Filter: If an incoming GPS point implies speed greater than 18 km/h (e.g. user got into a car or GPS jumped across cell towers), the delta is ignored to preserve pure walking metrics.

3.3 MET-Based Calorie Estimation (CalorieService)

Concept: Many apps guess calories using generic step counts. WalkMate uses the Compendium of Physical Activities, the gold standard in exercise physiology developed by Stanford and the American College of Sports Medicine.

What is a MET? MET stands for Metabolic Equivalent of Task. 1.0 MET is the rate of energy spent sitting quietly at rest (approx. $1\text{ kcal} / \text{kg} / \text{hr}$).

Calories per minute = (MET × 3.5 × Weight_in_kg) / 200
Calories per second = Calories_per_minute / 60

3.4 500m Milestone Cheers & Insights (MilestoneService)

Concept: Traditional trackers wait until 1 km or 5 km to notify you. For a beginner or stressed individual, 1 km can feel far. Every 500 meters (0.5 km), WalkMate triggers a celebratory micro-banner:

1.0 km 🌿 "One full kilometer! Feel the earth gently supporting your stride." Fresh oxygen circulating to the brain's hippocampus.
1.5 km 🍃 "You've settled into a lovely rhythm. Notice the quiet around you." Neck and shoulder tightness naturally decompressing.
2.0 km ✨ "Two kilometers of self-care. Notice how your thoughts clear." ~100 kcal burned: powers 2 hours of sharp cognitive focus.
3.0 km 🌾 "Three full kilometers. Beautiful dedication to your well-being." Endorphin release creates gentle, lasting mood elevation.

3.5 The Breathing Pulse Circle (PulseCircle)

Concept: During an active walk, the center circle doesn't just show distance—it gently expands and contracts in a 3.2-second smooth sine curve. This timing matches the physiological box-breathing cadence, subconsciously guiding the user to breathe deeply and release stress while walking.

3.6 The Polaroid Walk Memory Card (MemoryCardService)

Concept: When you finish a walk, you shouldn't just see a boring spreadsheet of numbers. You created a memory!

  • Editorial Polaroid Layout: Off-white textured background, subtle warm shadows, rounded photo frame, typography with serif accents.
  • Photo Options: Take a photo with Camera or pick from Gallery via image_picker. If no photo is chosen, it automatically renders a calming botanical tree illustration with sun rays.
  • RepaintBoundary Technology: Flutter can render any widget subtree directly into an off-screen bitmap at 3.0x pixel ratio (Retina print quality).
  • Share & Download: The bitmap is written as a PNG to temporary storage and opened directly in Android's native share tray (WhatsApp, Instagram Stories, Telegram, or Gallery).

5. How to Run, Test, and Deploy

A. Live Interactive Simulator (Already Running!)

You can run and test all screens, walk simulations, 500m milestones, and memory card exports right now in your browser at:
http://localhost:3456

B. Running in Android Studio

  1. Open Android Studio.
  2. Click Open and choose the folder: Runwithme.
  3. In the device selector at the top bar, pick your installed emulator (Pixel_4 or Medium_Phone_API_36).
  4. Click the green Run ▶ button. Android Studio will automatically build the debug APK and launch it on your virtual phone!

C. Testing on Your Physical Android Phone

  1. On your Android phone, go to Settings > About Phone and tap Build Number 7 times to enable Developer Options.
  2. Go to Developer Options and toggle USB Debugging to ON.
  3. Plug your phone into your PC via USB cable and allow the debugging prompt on your phone screen.
  4. In terminal, run: flutter run. The app installs directly onto your phone!
